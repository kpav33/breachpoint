Top-down tactical shooter · Phase 8 design system

Designing with the darkness.

Breachpoint is a round-based bomb-defuse shooter seen from directly above, played through a 110° vision cone under heavy fog of war. Readability is the top value, juice second, decoration a distant third. This document is the specification for that look — tokens you can paste into config, one committed identity system, and pixel-accurate HUD mockups at the game's native 1280×720.

Palette & tokens

Named roles, not one-off hexes. Two fog directions ship as a set — the world, players and HUD are identical between them; only the darkness and its temperature change. Every world color is tuned to survive the fog multiplier and to stay legible for common color-vision deficiencies.

02

Faction & identity

Decision: fixed faction colors. Terrorists are always orange, Counter-Terrorists always blue — the same on every client, which is essential for the planned online multiplayer where "my team" isn't universal. Individuals within a team share the faction hue and are told apart by name label, not by tint. "You" reads through a white outline ring, never a unique color.

▸ WHICH ONE IS ME
A 3px white outline ring plus a white direction notch. The ring is legible over lit floor and at the fog edge, and adds zero new hue to parse mid-firefight.
▸ FACING
A notch cut into the circle's rim in body color for others, white for you — the single most-read piece of information in the game (who is looking where).
▸ HEALTH
A 4px bar above the head, green→amber→red by value. Only drawn when HP < 100, so full-health players stay clean.
▸ CARRYING THE BOMB
A bright objective-orange square clipped to the circle's shoulder. Objective orange is yellower than T body orange so the tag never dissolves into a T carrier.
03

Typography

One superfamily, two roles. IBM Plex Mono carries every ticking number — its fixed advance means timers, money and ammo never reflow as digits change, and it's already tabular. IBM Plex Sans Condensed carries banners and labels, packing loud words into narrow HUD real estate. Both are on Google Fonts, both web-loadable, both keep the "clean, dry, tactical" tone.

Component specs

The repeatable pieces, with the rules behind them. Every panel shares one recipe: rgba(10,13,17,0.82) fill, 1px #2A323B border, 6–10px radius — so nothing on the HUD looks like it came from a different game.

World styling & effects

Richness comes from a few cheap, flat treatments — a top-edge highlight, site striping, spawn markings — never from texture. Every one survives the fog multiplier because it's built from the same tokens the fog already knows how to dim.

Wall — top-edge highlight
A 3–4px lighter cap on the north edge + a dark right shadow fakes height cheaply and shows which side is cover.
A
Bombsite floor
Warm tint + faint 45° striping + a dashed boundary + the giant 15%-alpha letter. Reads as "objective ground" even at fog edge.
